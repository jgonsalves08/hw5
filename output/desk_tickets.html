In most cases that makes more sense, because the Boss sees the whole ticket. On tools, I expected check_overdue, check_price and list_tickets, but they weren't used. 3What would have been simpler as one agent with tools — and why?

Any ticket where the agents weren't really working together would have been simpler as one agent. On tickets 101 and 103 the Boss called each specialist one after another and simply passed facts along. No specialist ever needed to talk to another. One agent holding all the tools would have done the same steps (check stock → check cash/price → write the draft) with fewer model calls. It also wouldn't have to re-explain the ticket in every hand-off. Ticket 103 is the clearest case: it's a straight line from stock, to price, to reply.

The back-and-forth on ticket 102 also wouldn't have happened with one agent. Facilities and Accounting re-checked each other's work, which made 102 the slowest and most expensive run (19 model requests and 36k input tokens, versus 14–16 requests and 26–29k for the others).

I still think splitting the work into multiple agents makes sense for a real business, because the split is also a safety boundary:

  • Only Accounting can queue a payment.
  • Customer Service never sees cash or costs, so it can't easily leak them (and on 103 it still almost did).
  • Facilities checking the rent notice separately from the agent that queues the payment is a real fraud check.

The better design is probably a middle ground: one agent for simple, linear tickets, and the team for tickets that touch money, plus a rule so the Boss doesn't send the same question to two agents at once.

Evidence: hand-off lists and run stats (model requests, tokens, time) at the bottom of each Actual section on 101, 102 and 103.

4Three new problems this agent team could solve with the tools I built

a) A customer orders something that is in stock

Example: a student asks for 2 Yale Caps (CC-HAT-BLUE, 40 on hand, $22 list). Inventory confirms stock with check_inventory and Accounting reads the price with check_price. Customer Service drafts a "ready for pickup in Aisle C" reply with draft_customer_message. No money moves and no approval is needed. It's the simple, happy-path version of ticket 101.

b) Restocking a sold-out item from a vendor with no unpaid invoices

When something sells out, the team can handle the whole restock up to the approval step:

  • Inventory spots the zero with check_inventory.
  • It picks the vendor whose specialty fits with list_vendors, and confirms the vendor has no unpaid invoices, so it will ship. It reads the lead time and ETA from the same tool.
  • Accounting checks the margin with check_price and the cash with get_cash_balance, then queues the order with prepare_purchase_order for a human to approve.

It's the same flow as ticket 101, but because the vendor isn't blocked, nothing has to be paid first. Example: the Crest Mug (CC-MUG-CREST) has 0 on hand. Inventory finds Elm City Gifts ("mugs and small goods", 3-day lead time, nothing unpaid), and Accounting sees a healthy margin ($3.50 cost vs. $14 list) before queuing the restock.

c) A suspicious or wrong rent notice

Example: an email claims rent is $2,800, or asks for payment to a different landlord. Facilities compares it with the lease using list_leases, check_overdue and list_payments, sees the mismatch (or that September rent is already paid as payment #2), and refuses to recommend payment. Even if someone pushed it through, prepare_payment takes the amount from the lease, not the email, and refuses duplicates. This is the fraud-check side of ticket 102.

5Three new problems this agent team could not solve — and what it would need

a) Bringing money in: sales and customer payments

The app is built so that cash only goes out, which is why Cash ends at $160 and ticket 103 couldn't fund the 12 extra hoodies. Even if the Yale AI Club accepted the $49 offer, nothing could record the $392 sale of the 8 in-stock hoodies, take the payment or lower the inventory. Needs: a Sales Agent with create_order, record_customer_payment (human-confirmed deposit into cash_accounts) and reserve_or_ship_inventory tools, plus a new orders table. Accounting would then see the incoming cash before deciding on a restock.

b) Receiving shipments and closing the loop with customers and vendors

On ticket 101 we paid the $840 invoice so the tee reprint could ship, but when it arrives around 2026-09-05, nothing updates inventory. Tauhid's reply is still only a draft, and the team can't read a vendor's or customer's response. Needs: a Receiving / Communications Agent with:

  • receive_shipment (adds stock to inventory)
  • track_vendor_order
  • send_message, which only sends a draft after a human clicks Send
  • an inbox reader that turns new emails into tickets

c) Planning ahead for next month's bills

After ticket 102, the lease's next due date is 2026-10-02 for another $2,400, but checking holds $160. Today the team can only react: prepare_payment would refuse for lack of cash when the ticket arrives. It can't forecast the gap, schedule payments or suggest options. The desk's "today" also never moves forward. Needs: a Finance / Planning Agent with:

  • cash_forecast (upcoming bills vs. expected sales)
  • schedule_payment (pay on the due date, still human-approved)
  • a way to advance desk.date_today
  • a tool to propose financing or negotiate a payment plan for a human to approve
